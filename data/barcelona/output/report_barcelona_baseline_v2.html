Geographic Distribution

The screening retained 47 Food Sharing Initiatives distributed across all ten Barcelona districts represented by the page-stated locations and the supplied coordinate records. Eixample has 9 retained records, the largest district group, while Sants-Montjuïc and Ciutat Vella each have 7. Sant Martí contains 6 retained initiatives and Gràcia 5, followed by Sarrià-Sant Gervasi with 4, Nou Barris with 3, and Sant Andreu, Horta-Guinardó and Les Corts with 2 each. The submitted coordinates show a dense central belt where projects such as DiSA Sagrada Família, Foodcoop BCN, El Guaret and La Garangola sit in or around Eixample. The eastern cluster includes Casa de Llavors, CoopeClot, Hort del Clot and La Unió de Poblenou - La Datzira in Sant Martí, combining seed sharing, collective purchasing and community growing.

The south-western cluster is anchored by Can Batlló Cuina, La Garrofera de Sants, Panxacontenta and Can Batlló Xarxa d'Aliments in Sants-Montjuïc, with Hort Urbà Espai Colònia Castells and Consum - Programa Profit extending the retained set into Les Corts. North of the centre, Can Valent and Userda 9 are located in Nou Barris, while Hort Comunitari Pla i Armengol and Hort del Mercat represent Horta-Guinardó. Ciutat Vella combines neighbourhood food co-operation and gardens with social support, including La Sardineta, Les Horteres de la Ribera, Fundació Roure and Sant'Egidio - Comedor para los Pobres. The coordinate map plots 45 of the 47 retained records because the submitted rows for Esperança Barcelona and the Ateneu L'Harmonia Nevera Solidària contain no latitude-longitude pair, although their visited pages provide Barcelona locations.

Types of Food Sharing Initiatives

Food co-operatives are the largest primary type, accounting for 19 of 47 retained initiatives (40.4%). Community gardens and seed-sharing projects account for 11 retained initiatives (23.4%). Food aid, surplus redistribution and community-fridge initiatives account for 10 records (21.3%), while social meals and community kitchens account for 5 (10.6%). The remaining 2 initiatives (4.3%) are cooperative or social food-retail models, represented by Foodcoop BCN and L'Economat Social. The co-operative group includes neighbourhood organisations such as Les Trementinaires del Coll-Vallcarca, which distributes orders from ecological and proximity producers, and Userda 9, whose members jointly order directly from selected producers.

Community-growing projects range from the 1,215-square-metre Hort Comunitari Pla i Armengol to the educational Hort Comunitari de La Barceloneta, whose page describes regular workshops, monthly assemblies and Saturday work sessions. Seed exchange is explicit at Casa de Llavors, while the Barceloneta garden also reports obtaining seeds through exchanges with neighbouring gardens and donations. Redistribution models range from the communal fridges at Nevera Solidària Espai210 and Nevera Solidària - Ateneu L'Harmonia to organised recovery-and-delivery systems such as Nutrició Sense Fronteres - Comparteix el Menjar and Social Fooding. Prepared-food sharing is represented by recurring communal meals at Can Batlló Cuina, street outreach by Esperança Barcelona, and social dining services operated by Emmaús Menjador Social, Fundació Catalunya La Pedrera - Come en compañía and Sant'Egidio - Comedor para los Pobres.

Operational and Funding Models

Using one primary operating model per retained record, 32 of 47 initiatives are community-led, 8 are NGO-led, 5 are council-supported and 2 are classified as commercial or market-operating models. Community leadership is especially prominent among food co-operatives, including 30 Panxes, Les Trementinaires del Coll-Vallcarca, La Cebolla Roja and Userda 9, whose pages describe member participation, collective ordering or self-management. At Can Batlló Cuina, volunteers manage the kitchen commission and the wider Can Batlló site describes itself as a neighbourhood self-managed space, placing its food activity firmly in the community-led group. Council support is clearest at Benet Corner, whose 420-square-metre site was ceded by Barcelona City Council, and at Hort Urbà Espai Colònia Castells, where the district funded the initial garden infrastructure while a local association manages the space. The publicly owned land described by Hort Comunitari Pla i Armengol and the district project managed by the non-profit cooperative Tarpuna at Hort del Mercat provide two further examples of municipal support combined with community or non-profit management.

The NGO-led group includes DiSA Sagrada Família, Emmaús Menjador Social, Fundació Catalunya La Pedrera - Come en compañía, Fundació Roure, Nutrició Sense Fronteres - Comparteix el Menjar, Sant'Egidio - Comedor para los Pobres, Social Fooding and Aspasim - Un hort per a tothom. The Nutrició Sense Fronteres - Comparteix el Menjar page also displays support from public and philanthropic institutions, showing that NGO operation can coexist with external grants and institutional backing rather than relying on a single funding source. Foodcoop BCN remains classified as community-led because its page states that members own the supermarket and make management and purchasing decisions, even though the same page also shows external financing and European NextGenerationEU support. No retained page supports describing Barcelona's FSI landscape as uniformly government-funded; the visited sources instead document a mixed ecology of self-management, non-profit provision, municipal support and a small number of commercial or cooperative retail mechanisms.

Reach and Activity

Several retained pages publish concrete participation evidence: Les Trementinaires del Coll-Vallcarca reports 29 family units and 23 food producers, with order distribution every Wednesday from 18:00 to 21:00. Userda 9 reports about 25 member households, weekly orders to producers and Wednesday collection, while Pinyol Vermell describes a collective of 30 family units. Demand for plots at Hort de la Font Trobada is directly visible in its waiting-list posts: a 15-person list filled in less than a week in July 2024, and the project reports typical annual rotation of five to ten people across seven years of experience. Foodcoop BCN lists more than 1,200 products and asks member-consumers to participate for roughly two to two-and-a-half hours per month, while also remaining open to non-members at public prices. At Can Batlló Cuina, activity is weekly: a cooking workshop is held on Tuesday afternoons and a Thursday menu uses seasonal and rescued food for members and neighbourhood residents to eat together.

Esperança Barcelona states that its volunteer outreach has operated since November 2013 and runs two routes each week, on Saturday and Sunday afternoons, distributing homemade soup and sandwiches to people experiencing homelessness. Sant'Egidio - Comedor para los Pobres reports opening two days per week and records more than 1,500 people welcomed with a hot meal during its first five years, with about 300 new people per year during that period. The current Nutrició Sense Fronteres - Comparteix el Menjar homepage reports 650,403 kilograms of recovered food and 1.3 million meals delivered since the Comparteix el Menjar programme began in 2012. Where retained pages provide schedules, waiting lists, membership figures or cumulative outputs, those measures are reported directly here; older cooperative blogs without current counts are not assigned estimated reach or popularity.

Digital Presence and Accessibility

The screening ledger covers all 261 submitted URLs and retains 47, while 94 are classified as inaccessible, 64 as not an FSI, 31 as directory or policy pages, 17 as outside the city, 5 as duplicates and 3 as ended activities. The inaccessible category includes dead links, anti-bot or login barriers and social-media pages that did not expose usable page content during the visit, so it should not be interpreted as proof that the underlying organisation has ceased to exist. Among retained projects, Hort Comunitari Pla i Armengol has posts dated June 2026 and La Sardineta has posts dated September 2025, providing unusually clear evidence of recent web activity. El Guaret also carried a June 2025 recruitment post, while Can Valent had a 2025 post, giving contemporary signals for initiatives in Eixample and Nou Barris. In contrast, sites such as Les Horteres de la Ribera and La Unió de Poblenou - La Datzira are dominated by older blog material, so their pages establish the initiative model and location more strongly than they establish present-day participation volume.

Operational accessibility is particularly clear on Foodcoop BCN, which publishes its street address, opening hours, membership option, public-shopping option and online-delivery information. Nevera Solidària - Ateneu L'Harmonia similarly publishes the Ateneu's address and opening hours alongside the Nevera Solidària description, while Can Batlló Cuina provides contact channels and recurring activity days. Municipal network pages, urban-garden guides and food-policy resources were deliberately excluded from initiative counts because they describe systems, directories or programmes rather than one operating FSI at the submitted URL. The final ledger preserves every submitted URL in source order, making inaccessible and excluded records visible instead of silently removing them from the evidence base.

Notable Initiatives

Nutrició Sense Fronteres - Comparteix el Menjar is notable for scale and logistics: its current site describes recovery of surplus food from hotels, restaurants, caterers, schools and supermarkets for social organisations in Barcelona and the metropolitan area, supported by food-safety protocols and refrigerated transport. Foodcoop BCN is distinctive as Barcelona's first cooperative, participatory and non-profit supermarket according to its own page, with member ownership, member voting and shopping access also available to the wider public. Hort de la Font Trobada stands out for documented demand, because its 15-place waiting list filled in less than a week and its rules restrict applications to Poble-sec residents, tying participation closely to the neighbourhood. Can Batlló Cuina combines food rescue with social eating rather than only distribution, using rescued and seasonal food for a recurring Thursday meal and running a weekly cooking workshop. The two retained Nevera Solidària pages show different neighbourhood implementations of community-fridge practice: Nevera Solidària Espai210 in Sagrada Família and Nevera Solidària - Ateneu L'Harmonia in Sant Andreu de Palomar, the latter describing its project as the first solidarity fridge in Catalonia when launched in 2016.

Les Trementinaires del Coll-Vallcarca is a particularly transparent consumption cooperative because it reports 29 family units, 23 producers, a weekly distribution window and six work commissions. Hort Comunitari Pla i Armengol is notable for its 1,215-square-metre publicly owned community garden and for recent 2026 web activity, while Benet Corner demonstrates a different council-supported model on a 420-square-metre site in Ciutat Vella. Direct meal support is represented by two contrasting long-running models: Esperança Barcelona takes homemade food into street outreach routes, whereas Sant'Egidio - Comedor para los Pobres operates a fixed dining room near Barcelona Cathedral two days per week. Fundació Roure and DiSA Sagrada Família illustrate the overlap between food sharing and broader social support, with Fundació Roure listing a community pantry and meals-oriented services in Ciutat Vella and DiSA distributing food and basic necessities free of charge to people referred through social services.
