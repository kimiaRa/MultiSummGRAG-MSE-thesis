## Set A -- label validation (validates E1's classifier)

The sample was deliberately weighted toward the error-prone classes (6 AUDITABLE_DIRECT / 10 AUDITABLE_DERIVED / 4 NOT_MACHINE_AUDITABLE, rather than proportional to their true population sizes), so a pooled agreement figure or pooled kappa would not estimate population-level agreement and is not reported. Agreement is reported per stratum instead.

**Provenance note:** the hidden file's `label` column uses an older token set (SUPPORTED/DERIVED/UNSUPPORTED) than the current E1 taxonomy (AUDITABLE_DIRECT/AUDITABLE_DERIVED/NOT_MACHINE_AUDITABLE) the human annotated against. The rename SUPPORTED->AUDITABLE_DIRECT, DERIVED->AUDITABLE_DERIVED, UNSUPPORTED->NOT_MACHINE_AUDITABLE is confirmed exactly (not assumed): the withheld per-class counts (411/52/172) match current e1_claims.csv label counts one-for-one, so this is a straight rename, not a reclassification. Also: the file named at task time, `e5_set_a_reduced.csv`, has MY_LABEL blank for all 20 rows; the real annotations (same 20 item_ids) were used from `e5_set_a.csv` instead.

**Redo note (2026-08-14):** the first pass gave 0/4 agreement on AUDITABLE_DIRECT. All 4 of those items are baseline claims, and the annotation instruction had wrongly pointed the annotator at `facts_<city>.json` (the pipeline's evidence base) instead of the screening ledger, which is the baseline's actual evidence base. The instruction was corrected and the 14 baseline items in the sample were re-annotated (`e5_set_a_redo.csv`) against explicit per-row ledger counts; the 6 pipeline items were not re-annotated, since the defect never touched them. First-pass and second-pass agreement are reported side by side below and the first-pass numbers are kept, not deleted -- the *change* (or lack of it) between the two passes is itself the evidence for whether the protocol-defect explanation holds.
